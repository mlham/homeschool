# Lesson: Break Apart Multiplication (3rd Grade)

## Welcome, Math Detective

Today we will use the **break apart rule** to solve multiplication puzzles. When a problem feels big, we split it into smaller parts we already know. Then we put those parts back together for the final answer. It is just like building a LEGO ship: small bricks first, then the whole ship!

## What Is the Break Apart Rule?

Break apart one number into tens and ones (or another friendly split), multiply each part, and add or subtract the smaller answers. Grown-ups call this the distributive property, but we will stick with the break apart rule.

## Example Mission: 8 × 8

### Trick A: 8 as 5 + 3

We can split one of the eights into 5 and 3.

```text
8 × 8 = 8 × (5 + 3)
      = (8 × 5) + (8 × 3)
      = 40 + 24
      = 64
```

### Trick B: 8 as 10 - 2

You can also split it into a bigger number minus a smaller number.

```text
8 × 8 = 8 × (10 - 2)
      = (8 × 10) - (8 × 2)
      = 80 - 16
      = 64
```

### Trick C: Double It

Split the other way by making two equal parts.

```text
8 × 8 = (8 × 4) + (8 × 4)
      = 32 + 32
      = 64
```

No matter which trick you pick, you land on **64**. That is the power of breaking apart!

## Why the Rule Works

When you break a number into pieces, each piece is easier to multiply. After solving the mini problems, add or subtract the results. Just like combining small LEGO builds into one strong model, the break apart rule helps you build big answers from little blocks.
